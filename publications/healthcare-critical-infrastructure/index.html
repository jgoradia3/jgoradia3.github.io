Building Secure and Trustworthy AI for Healthcare Critical Infrastructure

Pooja Walia · Rajat Rawal · Jay Goradia

Read on IEEE Xplore ↗Read on ResearchGate ↗Cite this paper ↓

What problem does this paper address?

Healthcare AI faces connected security, privacy, and operational risks. The paper examines how protections can fit into one architecture rather than operate as isolated measures.

Contribution and approach

The contribution is architectural synthesis: organizing established techniques into four complementary layers.

LayerRole in the proposed architecture
DataIdentity, integrity, and provenance
Model and trainingPrivacy-preserving collaboration and robustness
SystemDigital-twin simulation and monitoring
GovernanceExplainability and auditability

Evidence and limitations

A hospital device anomaly-detection scenario illustrates the design. It is not a new algorithm or comparative experimental benchmark; operational validation remains future work.

The components have different boundaries: privacy protections do not eliminate poisoning, explanations do not establish robustness, and simulations depend on model fidelity.

When this paper is relevant to cite

Use it for discussion of integrated healthcare AI security architectures, complementary controls, or system-level trust. Use primary experimental studies for measured effectiveness claims.
