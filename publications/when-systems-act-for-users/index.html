When Systems Act for Users

Jay Goradia

Read on ACM Interactions ↗Cite this article ↓

What problem does this article address?

A single user request can trigger actions by multiple agents and services. People may see an outcome without understanding which actor acted, what permission it used, or how to intervene. The article examines that gap as an interaction-design problem.

Contribution and design approach

The article connects delegated agency with four design concerns:

  • Identity legibility: make the actors behind meaningful actions understandable.
  • Intent visibility: show how the system interpreted the request.
  • Bounded delegation: communicate what access applies, for which task, and for how long.
  • Intervention and recovery: explain available pause, reversal, and escalation options.

It discusses interface patterns such as an intent preview, an activity trail, a permission boundary, and recovery controls.

Evidence and limitations

This is a design argument supported by prior literature and illustrative scenarios. It does not report a controlled user study or experimentally establish that the proposed patterns improve trust or safety.

Interfaces need reliable information from authorization and execution systems. Showing an activity trail alone does not enforce permissions, and some completed actions cannot be reversed.

When this article is relevant to cite

Use it when discussing delegated agency, understandable agent permissions, or human intervention in multi-actor interfaces. For quantitative usability outcomes or authorization guarantees, cite studies that directly evaluate those claims.
